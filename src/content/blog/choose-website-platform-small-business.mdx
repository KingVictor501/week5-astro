Choosing a website platform is one of the first decisions in building a site, and one of the hardest to undo. The right choice depends on your budget, your technical comfort, how often you'll update content, and how much performance and flexibility you need. Here's how to choose a website platform for a small business without regretting it later.

<Callout title="TL;DR">
  Website builders are fastest and simplest but less flexible. Content management systems offer more control and many extensions but need maintenance. Custom-built sites deliver the best performance and flexibility but need a developer. Choose based on who will maintain the site, not just launch cost.
</Callout>

## Start with your requirements

Before comparing platforms, list what your site needs to do:

- How many pages, now and in a few years?
- Do you need online booking, a store, or member areas?
- Who will update content, and how often?
- How important are speed and search visibility?
- What's your budget for launch and for ongoing costs?

The U.S. Small Business Administration's guide to [marketing and sales](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/manage-your-business/marketing-sales) is a useful reminder that your website should serve your broader marketing goals, not the other way around.

## Option 1: Website builders

Website builders are all-in-one services. Hosting, templates, and editing tools come together, usually for a monthly fee.

**Good for:** very small sites, owners who want to edit everything themselves, and tight timelines.

**Pros:**

- Quick to launch with drag-and-drop editing
- Hosting, security, and updates handled for you
- Predictable monthly pricing

**Cons:**

- Less control over code, performance, and design details
- Can be harder to move to another platform later
- Advanced features may require paid add-ons

## Option 2: Content management systems

A content management system (CMS) separates content from design and supports themes and plugins for extra features. Some are self-hosted, meaning you choose your own hosting.

**Good for:** sites with regular content updates, blogs, and businesses that want more flexibility.

**Pros:**

- Large ecosystems of themes and extensions
- Flexible and widely supported by developers
- Easy content editing once set up

**Cons:**

- Requires regular updates for security
- Too many plugins can slow the site down
- Quality varies widely between themes and plugins

## Option 3: Custom-built sites

Custom sites are built with modern frameworks and tailored to your needs. Content can live in files or a headless CMS that gives you an easy editing interface.

**Good for:** businesses that want top performance, unique design, strong accessibility, and room to grow.

**Pros:**

- Fast, lightweight pages that score well on Core Web Vitals
- Full control over design, accessibility, and SEO
- No unnecessary features weighing the site down

**Cons:**

- Needs a developer to build and change structural features
- Higher upfront investment in most cases

Google's [Web Vitals guidance](https://web.dev/articles/vitals) explains why lightweight, fast pages matter for both visitors and search.

## Compare the total cost of ownership

Launch price is only part of the picture. Consider over several years:

- Monthly platform, hosting, and plugin fees
- Time spent on updates and maintenance
- Cost of fixing problems or security issues
- Cost of moving platforms later if you outgrow the first choice

## Questions to ask before deciding

1. Will I own my content and domain, and can I export them?
2. Can I edit everyday content myself?
3. How fast will pages load on a phone?
4. How does the platform handle accessibility and SEO basics?
5. What happens if I need a feature it doesn't offer?

## A simple decision guide

- **Need a basic site quickly and want to do it yourself?** A website builder.
- **Publish often and want flexibility with a large ecosystem?** A content management system.
- **Want the fastest, most tailored site that can grow with you?** A custom build with an easy editing setup.
