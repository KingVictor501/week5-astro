Great content can't rank if Google can't find, load, or understand your pages. The good news is that the most common technical SEO problems on small business websites are easy to spot, and you can check for them in about half an hour with free tools.

Work through the steps below in order. Make a note of anything that looks wrong; you don't have to fix everything today.

## Before you start

You'll need two free tools:

- **Google Search Console**, connected to your website. If you haven't set it up yet, do that first. It's the only place Google tells you directly how it sees your site.
- **PageSpeed Insights** (pagespeed.web.dev), which tests how quickly your pages load on phones and computers.

<Callout title="No Search Console yet?">
  Setting it up usually takes a few minutes and requires verifying that you own the site. It's worth doing even if you skip everything else on this list.
</Callout>

## Step 1: Check that Google can index your pages (10 minutes)

In Search Console, open the **Pages** report under Indexing. It shows how many of your pages are indexed and lists the reasons others aren't.

Look for:

- **Important pages marked "not indexed."** Your service pages and homepage should all be indexed.
- **"Excluded by noindex tag"** on pages you want in search. A leftover noindex tag from when the site was being built is a surprisingly common problem.
- **"Blocked by robots.txt."** Your robots.txt file shouldn't block pages you want to rank.

Then use the **URL Inspection** tool at the top of Search Console to test your most important page. It tells you whether that page is on Google and whether Google can crawl it.

### Check for a sitemap

Under **Sitemaps** in Search Console, confirm you've submitted a sitemap and that Google read it successfully. A sitemap doesn't guarantee indexing, but it helps Google discover every page you care about.

## Step 2: Test your page speed on mobile (10 minutes)

Most small business visitors arrive on a phone, so test the mobile version first. Run your homepage and one key service page through PageSpeed Insights.

Focus on the **Core Web Vitals** section at the top:

- **Largest Contentful Paint (LCP):** how quickly the main content appears.
- **Interaction to Next Paint (INP):** how quickly the page responds when someone taps or clicks.
- **Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS):** whether things jump around as the page loads.

You don't need to understand every recommendation. The usual culprits on small business sites are oversized images, too many third-party scripts (chat widgets, trackers, sliders), and slow, inexpensive hosting.

## Step 3: Review titles, descriptions, and headings (5 minutes)

Search for `site:yourdomain.com` on Google to see your pages the way searchers do. Check that:

- Every page has a **unique title** that says what the page is about, not just your business name.
- Descriptions are written for people and make them want to click.
- No important page shows a generic title like "Home" or "Untitled."

Then open your main service page and check that it has **one H1 heading** that clearly describes the service, followed by logical H2 subheadings.

## Step 4: Spot-check mobile usability and security (5 minutes)

Open your site on your own phone and try to do the one thing you want visitors to do, like calling you or filling in a form. If it's fiddly for you, it's fiddly for customers.

Finally, confirm your site loads on **https://** with a padlock in the browser and that the http:// version redirects to it. An insecure site can put visitors off before they read a word.

## What to do with your notes

You'll likely end up with a short list of issues. Prioritize them like this:

1. Anything stopping important pages from being indexed.
2. Slow mobile speed on your key pages.
3. Missing or duplicate titles and headings.
4. Everything else.

If the list is longer than you have time for, or the fixes involve code or hosting changes, a technical SEO audit can turn it into a clear, prioritized plan.
