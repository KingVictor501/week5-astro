Getting visitors to your website is only half the job. The other half is turning them into calls, bookings, and inquiries. Website conversion optimization doesn't require a redesign. Often a few focused changes to your most visited pages make a noticeable difference. Here are seven to start with.

<Callout title="TL;DR">
  Make your headline say what you do and for whom, put one clear call to action above the fold, make contact options obvious on mobile, shorten your forms, add real proof near the call to action, speed up the page, and track what changes so you can keep improving.
</Callout>

## 1. Write a headline that explains what you do

Visitors decide quickly whether they're in the right place. A vague headline like "Welcome to Our Website" wastes that moment. Say what you do, who it's for, and where:

- Vague: "Excellence in Every Project"
- Clear: "Kitchen and Bathroom Remodeling in Springfield"

Add a short supporting line that explains the main benefit or what makes you different.

## 2. Put one clear call to action above the fold

Decide on the single most important action for each page, such as booking a call, requesting a quote, or calling now. Make it a prominent button near the top, then repeat it further down. Use specific button text like "Get My Free Quote" rather than "Submit" or "Learn More."

People scan pages more than they read them, according to Nielsen Norman Group's research on [how people read on the web](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/website-reading/), so make the next step easy to spot.

## 3. Make contact options obvious on mobile

Most visitors browse on their phones. On mobile:

- Show a tap-to-call phone number near the top.
- Keep the main button visible without scrolling far.
- Make buttons large enough to tap easily.

For local service businesses, a phone call is often the highest-value lead. Don't make people hunt for your number.

## 4. Shorten your forms

Every field you add gives visitors another reason to stop. Ask only for what you need to respond: name, phone or email, and a short message. You can collect details on the follow-up call. If you need more information, consider a two-step form that asks the easiest questions first.

## 5. Add real proof near your call to action

People hesitate before contacting a business they don't know. Reduce that hesitation with genuine proof placed near your forms and buttons:

- Customer reviews and testimonials, shared with permission
- Photos of your real work and team
- Licenses, certifications, and memberships
- Guarantees or clear policies

Never invent reviews, results, or statistics. Real, modest proof is more convincing than exaggerated claims, and it keeps you on the right side of advertising rules.

## 6. Speed up the page

A slow page loses visitors before they see your offer. Compress images, remove unnecessary scripts, and test your key pages on mobile. Our guide on [how to speed up a slow small business website](/blog/speed-up-small-business-website) explains where to start.

## 7. Track, test, and improve

Make sure form submissions and calls are tracked as conversions so you can see whether changes help. Then make one change at a time and compare results over several weeks. Small, steady improvements add up.

## Where to start

Begin with the pages that get the most traffic and the most important pages for leads: usually your homepage, top service pages, and contact page. Apply these changes there first, then roll them out across the site.
