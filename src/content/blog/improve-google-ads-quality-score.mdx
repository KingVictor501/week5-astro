Two businesses can bid on the same keyword and pay very different amounts for the same position. A big part of the difference is relevance, which Google summarizes with Quality Score. If you want to improve your Google Ads Quality Score, you need to understand what it measures and focus on the three parts you can influence.

<Callout title="TL;DR">
  Quality Score is a 1–10 diagnostic based on expected click-through rate, ad relevance, and landing page experience. Improve it by grouping closely related keywords, writing ads that echo those keywords, and sending clicks to fast pages that deliver what the ad promised. Use it as a guide, not a goal.
</Callout>

## What Quality Score is

Quality Score is a keyword-level rating from 1 to 10 that Google shows in your account. According to Google's [Quality Score documentation](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118), it's a diagnostic tool that estimates how your ads compare with other advertisers for the same keyword. It isn't directly used in the ad auction, but it reflects the same relevance factors that influence your costs and ad positions.

It's built from three components, each rated "Above average," "Average," or "Below average":

1. **Expected click-through rate:** how likely people are to click your ad.
2. **Ad relevance:** how closely your ad matches the intent behind the search.
3. **Landing page experience:** how relevant and useful your page is to people who click.

## How to see your Quality Score

In Google Ads, open your keywords view and add the Quality Score columns, including the three component columns. Look for keywords with high spend and low scores. That's where improvements pay off most.

## Improving expected click-through rate

- **Put the keyword in a headline.** People click ads that clearly match what they searched.
- **Lead with a benefit.** Same-day service, free estimates, or a guarantee give people a reason to choose you.
- **Use assets.** Sitelinks, callouts, and call buttons make your ad larger and more useful.
- **Remove irrelevant searches.** Negative keywords stop your ad from showing for searches where nobody would click.

## Improving ad relevance

Ad relevance usually suffers when ad groups contain too many different keywords. One ad can't speak to all of them.

- Split broad ad groups into smaller, tightly themed ones.
- Write ads specifically for each group's keywords.
- Make sure the ad describes exactly what the searcher wants.

For example, separate "water heater repair" and "water heater installation" into different ad groups with different ads.

## Improving landing page experience

- Send each ad group to the most relevant page, not your homepage.
- Make the page's headline and content match the ad.
- Load fast on mobile.
- Make the next step easy, with clear contact options.
- Be transparent about who you are and what you offer.

Our guide to [Google Ads landing pages that turn clicks into leads](/blog/google-ads-landing-page-best-practices) covers each of these in detail.

## Don't chase the number

Quality Score is a diagnostic, not a business result. A keyword with a modest score that produces profitable leads is more valuable than a perfect score with no conversions. Use the components to spot problems, then judge success by cost per lead and conversions.

## A Quality Score review routine

1. Monthly, sort keywords by spend and check Quality Score components.
2. For keywords rated below average on ad relevance, tighten the ad group and rewrite ads.
3. For below-average landing page experience, improve or change the destination page.
4. For below-average expected CTR, test new headlines and add assets.
5. Re-check after a few weeks.
