Boosting a post can feel like advertising, but it rarely gets the results small businesses hope for. A planned campaign with a clear objective, a defined audience, and proper tracking works far better. This starter playbook for Facebook ads for small business walks you through a first campaign that's built to bring in leads.

<Callout title="TL;DR">
  Pick an objective that matches your goal, target a tight local audience, create a simple ad with a real photo and a clear offer, send people to a focused landing page or lead form, track conversions, and give the campaign a couple of weeks before judging it.
</Callout>

## Boosting vs. running a campaign

The "Boost" button is quick, but it offers limited control over objectives, placements, and optimization. Meta's Ads Manager gives you the full set of tools. For anything beyond a quick test, use Ads Manager.

## Step 1: Choose the right objective

Your objective tells the platform what to optimize for. For most small businesses:

- **Leads:** collect contact details through an instant form or your website
- **Traffic:** send people to a page, useful for content or awareness
- **Sales:** for businesses selling online with tracking in place
- **Awareness:** reach as many local people as possible

If your goal is inquiries, choose **Leads**. If you optimize for traffic, the system will find people who click, not necessarily people who contact you.

## Step 2: Define your audience

Tight targeting stops your budget leaking to people who can't become customers:

- **Location:** set a radius around your business or service area.
- **Age and demographics:** only if they genuinely matter for your offer.
- **Interests and behaviors:** start broad within your location and let the system learn, or add a few relevant interests.
- **Custom audiences:** upload customer lists or target website visitors, if you have the right tracking and consent.
- **Lookalike audiences:** reach people similar to your customers once you have enough data.

Pew Research Center's [social media fact sheet](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/social-media/) offers useful context on who uses Facebook, which helps you decide whether it's the right platform for your audience.

## Step 3: Set a budget and schedule

Start with a modest daily budget you're comfortable testing for at least two weeks. The system needs time and data to learn who responds. Avoid making big changes in the first few days.

## Step 4: Create ads that stop the scroll

Good small business ads are usually simple:

- **A real photo or short video** of your work, team, or product
- **A clear headline** stating what you offer
- **A specific benefit**, such as free estimates, fast booking, or a seasonal offer
- **One call to action**, like "Get Quote" or "Book Now"

Create two or three variations with different images or headlines so you can see what performs best. Never use fake reviews or misleading claims.

## Step 5: Choose where leads go

You have two main options:

- **Instant forms** keep people on the platform, which can increase volume. Add a question or two to improve lead quality.
- **Your website landing page** gives you more control and a better chance to explain your offer, but requires tracking.

Whichever you choose, follow up quickly. Leads go cold fast.

## Step 6: Track results properly

Install Meta's tracking on your website if you send traffic there, and tag links with UTM parameters so Google Analytics can attribute visits and leads correctly. Google's guide to [collecting campaign data with custom URLs](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10917952) explains how.

## Step 7: Review and adjust

After a couple of weeks:

- Compare cost per lead across ad variations.
- Pause the weakest ads and create new ones based on the winners.
- Check lead quality with your team, not just lead counts.
- Refresh images before people tire of seeing the same ad.

## Common mistakes

- Boosting posts with no clear objective
- Targeting too broad an area for a local business
- Sending traffic to a homepage instead of a focused page
- Judging results after a day or two
- Ignoring leads until days later
