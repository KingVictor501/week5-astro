On September 29, 2026, Meta launched **Muse for Small Business**, an AI agent designed to help owners run their business and find new customers. It's one of the clearest signs yet that AI agents for small business are moving from experiments to mainstream products. Here's what it does and how to decide whether it's right for you.

<Callout title="TL;DR">
  Muse for Small Business is Meta's AI agent for small business owners. It connects to tools like Shopify, QuickBooks, Stripe, Canva, Slack, and Notion, plus Instagram analytics, Facebook pages, and Meta ad accounts. It's free with usage limits, with paid subscriptions for more. Start small, limit permissions, and review its work before trusting it with customer-facing tasks.
</Callout>

## What Meta announced

According to [TechCrunch's report on the launch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/meta-is-expanding-its-ai-agent-muse-to-small-businesses/), Meta says Muse for Small Business can help owners run their business and find new customers by connecting to the software they already use for sales, operations, and marketing.

### Integrations

TechCrunch listed integrations with:

- **Commerce and payments:** Shopify, Stripe, Intuit QuickBooks
- **Productivity and files:** Slack, Notion, Asana, Box, Dropbox, Zoom, Granola
- **Design and building:** Canva, Figma, Lovable
- **Marketing and CRM:** Klaviyo, HighLevel

It can also connect to Instagram professional account analytics, Facebook pages, and Meta ad accounts, and is reported to understand what a business sells, how its brand sounds, and what customers ask about most.

### Pricing

Muse for Small Business is **free with usage limits**, and businesses can buy a subscription plan for more usage.

### Meta's pitch

Meta wrote: "They told us they're short on hours, not ideas. So we built Muse for Small Business to help get work done with the tools they already use."

## What an AI agent like this could help with

Because it connects to several business tools, an agent like Muse could potentially help with tasks such as:

- Drafting social posts and ad ideas in your brand voice
- Summarizing sales, customer questions, or campaign results
- Pulling information together across tools instead of switching between them
- Preparing marketing materials with design tools

What it can do in practice will depend on the specific features available to your account.

## Questions to ask before you connect your tools

An agent with access to your sales, accounting, and customer data is powerful, so evaluate it carefully:

1. **What permissions does it need?** Connect only the tools required for the tasks you want help with.
2. **What can it do on its own?** Understand which actions need your approval, especially anything that spends money or contacts customers.
3. **How is your data used?** Read the terms and privacy settings before connecting customer or financial data.
4. **Can you review its work?** Keep a human check on anything customer-facing.
5. **How will you measure value?** Track the time saved on specific tasks over a few weeks.

## A sensible way to start

- Begin with the free tier and one low-risk task, such as summarizing campaign results.
- Connect the minimum number of tools.
- Review every output for accuracy and brand voice.
- Expand only after it proves reliable and useful.
- Revisit connected tools every few months and remove any the agent no longer needs.

Treat the agent like a new team member on probation: give it limited access, check its work closely at first, and widen its responsibilities only as it earns your trust.
