For plumbers, dentists, law firms, contractors, and many other local businesses, the phone is where leads happen. Yet most marketing reports only count form submissions. If you're not tracking calls, you may be underestimating your best channels and cutting campaigns that actually work. Call tracking for small business fills that gap.

<Callout title="TL;DR">
  Call tracking uses unique phone numbers to show which marketing channels and campaigns generate calls. Start with Google Ads call tracking for ads, consider a call tracking service with dynamic numbers for your website, set a minimum call length to filter out noise, and follow privacy and recording laws.
</Callout>

## Why phone leads go missing

Standard analytics tools see what happens on your website: page views, clicks, and form submissions. When someone reads your page and then dials your number from another phone, or taps a number on a printed flyer, that connection is lost.

The result: channels that drive many calls look weak in reports, and you might cut exactly the marketing that keeps your phone ringing.

## How call tracking works

Call tracking assigns different phone numbers to different sources. When a call comes in on a particular number, you know where the caller found you. The calls still forward to your normal business line, so nothing changes for your team.

There are two main approaches.

### Static tracking numbers

Each offline or fixed source gets its own number: one for your vehicle wraps, one for a direct mail campaign, one for a directory listing. It's simple and works well for print and other offline marketing.

### Dynamic number insertion

For your website, a script swaps the phone number shown to each visitor based on how they arrived, such as organic search, paid search, or social. It can connect calls to specific campaigns and even keywords.

## Starting with Google Ads

If you run Google Ads, start there. You can track calls from call assets on your ads and calls from your website after an ad click. Google explains how to [track calls to a phone number on your website](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095883). Calls can be counted as conversions, which helps automated bidding find more callers.

## Choosing a call tracking service

For tracking across all channels, many businesses use a dedicated call tracking service. When comparing options, look for:

- Dynamic number insertion for your website
- Integration with Google Analytics 4 and Google Ads
- Local numbers in your area code
- Reporting by source, campaign, and keyword
- Optional call recording, with clear consent controls

## Set a minimum call length

Not every call is a lead. Wrong numbers, sales calls, and quick hang-ups add noise. Set a minimum duration, such as 60 seconds, for a call to count as a conversion. Review a sample of calls regularly to make sure the threshold makes sense for your business.

## Protect your local listings

Changing the phone number on your Google Business Profile or directory listings can create inconsistent information. If you use tracking numbers there, follow each platform's guidance, which often allows a tracking number as the primary number with your main number listed as an additional one.

## Use call data responsibly

- **Recording laws vary.** Some places require consent from everyone on the call. Announce recordings when required.
- **Limit access** to recordings and caller details to people who need them.
- **Keep data only as long as necessary.**

## Turn calls into insight

Once calls are tracked, add them to your monthly reporting alongside form leads. You may find a channel you were about to cut is one of your strongest. Call data also reveals peak calling times, helping you staff the phones when it matters most.
