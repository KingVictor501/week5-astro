A redesign is a chance to modernize your website, fix what isn't working, and win more leads. It's also a common way to lose search traffic overnight if old pages disappear without redirects. This website redesign checklist walks you through each stage so your new site launches smoothly.

<Callout title="TL;DR">
  Before redesigning, record what works on your current site, including top pages, rankings, and conversions. Plan new pages around goals, map every old URL to a new one, keep SEO elements intact, test thoroughly on real devices, then launch with redirects, analytics, and a sitemap, and monitor closely for several weeks.
</Callout>

## Phase 1: Understand your current site

Before changing anything, record what's working:

- List your **top pages by traffic and leads** from Google Analytics.
- Note the **search queries and pages** bringing visitors in Google Search Console.
- Record **conversion rates** for key pages and forms.
- Crawl the site to get a **full list of current URLs**.
- Save **current titles, descriptions, and headings** for important pages.
- Ask customers and staff **what's frustrating** about the current site.

This baseline protects what's valuable and shows whether the redesign improves results.

## Phase 2: Set goals and plan

- Define **what the redesign should achieve**, such as more leads or easier booking.
- Plan the **new sitemap** and decide which pages stay, merge, or go.
- Choose the **platform** based on who will maintain the site.
- Plan **calls to action** for every key page.
- Set **performance and accessibility targets** before design begins.

## Phase 3: Content

- Keep or improve **content on pages that already rank** instead of replacing it with thinner copy.
- Update outdated information, services, and photos.
- Write clear **titles and meta descriptions** for every page.
- Add **alt text** to images.

## Phase 4: Redirects and SEO

This phase decides whether you keep your search visibility.

- Create a **redirect map** pairing every old URL with its best new equivalent.
- Use **permanent (301) redirects** for moved pages.
- Avoid **redirect chains**, where one redirect leads to another.
- Keep **structured data** and update it if details change.
- Update **internal links** to point directly to new URLs.

Google's guide to [site moves and migrations](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes) explains best practices for changing URLs safely.

## Phase 5: Testing before launch

- Test on **several phones, tablets, and browsers**.
- Submit **every form** and confirm emails arrive.
- Check **speed** with PageSpeed Insights.
- Navigate with a **keyboard only** to catch accessibility issues.
- Click every **link and button**.
- Confirm **analytics and conversion tracking** are installed on the staging site, ready for launch.

## Phase 6: Launch day

- Remove any **"noindex" settings** or password protection used during development.
- Turn on **redirects**.
- Confirm **HTTPS** works across the site.
- Submit the **new sitemap** in Google Search Console.
- Test **key pages and forms** again on the live site.
- Spot-check **old URLs** to confirm they redirect correctly.

## Phase 7: After launch

- Watch **Search Console** for crawl and indexing errors over the following weeks.
- Compare **traffic and leads** with your baseline.
- Fix **broken links and missing redirects** as they appear.
- Gather **feedback** from customers and staff.

Some ranking fluctuation after a redesign is normal while Google processes changes. Large, lasting drops usually point to missing redirects or lost content.
