For many local businesses, the first thing a new customer sees isn't your website. It's your Google Business Profile: the panel with your hours, photos, reviews, and a button to call or get directions. Google Business Profile optimization is one of the highest-impact, lowest-cost marketing tasks a small business can do.

<Callout title="TL;DR">
  Claim and verify your profile, choose the most accurate primary category, keep your name, address, and phone number identical everywhere, add real photos, respond to every review, and update hours before holidays. Then check it monthly.
</Callout>

## Why your profile matters

Google says local results are based mainly on three factors: relevance, distance, and prominence. You can't change your distance from a searcher, but you can make your profile more relevant and more complete. Google's own [tips to improve your local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) confirm that complete, accurate information helps.

## Step 1: Claim and verify your profile

Search for your business name on Google Maps. If a profile exists, claim it. If not, create one. Verification proves you own the business, and unverified profiles can be edited by anyone. Verification methods vary, so follow the options Google offers you.

## Step 2: Get your core details right

These fields matter most:

- **Business name.** Use your real-world name only. Adding keywords, like "Smith Plumbing Best Plumber Denver," breaks Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) and can get your profile suspended.
- **Primary category.** This is one of the strongest signals. Choose the category that describes your core business, not everything you do.
- **Secondary categories.** Add a few that describe other main services.
- **Address or service area.** If customers visit you, show your address. If you travel to customers, set a service area and hide your address.
- **Phone number and website.** Use a local number and link to your most relevant page.

Keep your name, address, and phone number identical to your website and other listings. Small differences, like "Street" versus "St.," are usually fine, but different phone numbers or old addresses cause confusion.

## Step 3: Write a helpful business description

You have room for a short description. Explain what you do, who you serve, and what makes you different, in plain language. Mention your main services naturally, but don't stuff in keywords or include links.

## Step 4: Add services, products, and attributes

The services section lets you list what you offer, sometimes with descriptions. Attributes cover details like wheelchair access or online appointments. These help your profile match more specific searches and answer questions before customers call.

## Step 5: Upload real photos

Add your logo, a cover photo, exterior shots that help people recognize your building, interior photos, and photos of your work or team. Real photos build trust far more than stock images. Add new ones every month or two so your profile looks active.

## Step 6: Build and respond to reviews

Reviews influence both rankings and decisions. Ask happy customers for a review shortly after a job, using the review link Google provides. Respond to every review:

- Thank positive reviewers by name and mention what they appreciated.
- Answer negative reviews calmly, acknowledge the issue, and offer to resolve it offline.

Never buy reviews or offer incentives for them. It violates Google's policies and can backfire badly.

## Step 7: Use posts and keep hours current

Posts let you share offers, events, and updates directly on your profile. They're a simple way to show you're active. Most importantly, set special hours before holidays so customers don't arrive to a locked door.

## A monthly profile checklist

1. Respond to any new reviews.
2. Add a few new photos.
3. Publish a short post.
4. Check that hours, phone number, and website link are correct.
5. Review the questions section and answer new ones.
