You pay for every click on your Google Ads, but the landing page decides whether that click becomes a lead. A focused Google Ads landing page can make the same ad budget go much further, while a generic page quietly wastes it. Here's what a page built for ad traffic should include.

<Callout title="TL;DR">
  Match the page to the ad and keyword, lead with a clear headline and offer, make the next step obvious, keep the form short, add trust signals near the call to action, and make sure the page loads fast on a phone. One page per service beats one page for everything.
</Callout>

## Why the homepage isn't enough

Your homepage serves everyone: past customers, job seekers, people comparing you to competitors. Someone who searched "emergency water heater repair" and clicked your ad wants one thing. If they land on a page about everything you do, many will leave before finding it.

A dedicated landing page also improves your **landing page experience**, one of the components of Quality Score. Google's guide to [Quality Score](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118) explains how relevance and usefulness affect your ads.

## 1. Match the message

The page headline should echo the search and the ad. If your ad says "Same-Day Water Heater Repair in Springfield," the page should say that too. This "message match" reassures people they've landed in the right place.

## 2. Lead with a clear offer

Within seconds, a visitor should understand:

- What you offer
- Who it's for and where you serve
- Why choose you, such as speed, guarantees, or experience
- What to do next

Put this above the fold on mobile. People tend to scan pages rather than read them, so the first lines carry the most weight, as Nielsen Norman Group's research on [how people scan web pages](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/) shows.

## 3. Make the next step obvious

Choose one primary action and repeat it down the page: call, book, or request a quote. Use a button with specific text, like "Get My Free Quote," instead of "Submit." On mobile, a tap-to-call button is often the most valuable element on the page.

## 4. Keep the form short

Every extra field gives people a reason to quit. Ask only for what you need to follow up: name, phone or email, and a short message or service selection. You can gather details later.

## 5. Add trust signals near the call to action

People hesitate before handing over contact details. Reduce that hesitation with:

- Genuine customer reviews or testimonials, with permission
- Licenses, certifications, or memberships
- Guarantees or clear pricing information
- Photos of your real team and work

Never invent testimonials or results. Real proof, even if modest, is more persuasive and keeps you on the right side of advertising rules.

## 6. Remove distractions

Landing pages work best with fewer exits. Consider removing the full navigation menu, unrelated links, and sidebars so attention stays on the offer and the action.

## 7. Make it fast

Slow pages lose visitors before they see anything. Compress images, limit scripts, and test the page in PageSpeed Insights on mobile. Speed matters for every page, but especially for one you're paying to send traffic to.

## 8. Track and test

Make sure each form submission and call is tracked as a conversion. Our guide to [setting up Google Ads conversion tracking](/blog/google-ads-conversion-tracking-setup) covers how. Then test one change at a time, such as a new headline or shorter form, and compare results over a few weeks.

## A quick landing page checklist

1. Headline matches the ad and keyword
2. Offer and benefits visible without scrolling on mobile
3. One clear call to action, repeated
4. Form with three to five fields at most
5. Real trust signals near the form
6. Fast load time on a phone
7. Conversion tracking working
