If your monthly marketing review means logging into five platforms and copying numbers into a spreadsheet, a dashboard will save you hours. Looker Studio, Google's free reporting tool, can pull your data into one view that updates automatically. Here's how to build a simple Looker Studio marketing dashboard for a small business.

<Callout title="TL;DR">
  Decide on your KPIs first, connect GA4 and your ad platforms as data sources, add a date range control and scorecards for top KPIs, add charts for leads by channel and trends over time, keep it to one or two pages, then share view-only access with your team.
</Callout>

## What Looker Studio is

[Looker Studio](https://lookerstudio.google.com/) is a free tool for building reports and dashboards from many data sources. It connects natively to Google products like Google Analytics 4, Google Ads, Search Console, and Google Sheets. Google's [Looker Studio documentation](https://support.google.com/looker-studio/answer/6283323) covers every feature in depth.

## Step 1: Decide what the dashboard is for

Before opening Looker Studio, write down the questions your dashboard should answer, such as:

- How many leads did we get this month, and from which channels?
- What did each lead cost?
- Is website traffic growing?
- Which pages bring in the most leads?

Then choose five to eight KPIs that answer them. Our guide to [marketing KPIs every small business should track](/blog/marketing-kpis-for-small-business) can help.

## Step 2: Connect your data sources

Start a blank report and add data sources:

- **Google Analytics 4** for traffic, channels, and key events
- **Google Ads** for spend, clicks, and conversions
- **Search Console** for search queries and impressions
- **Google Sheets** for anything else, like offline sales or social ad spend

Each connector needs permission to access your accounts. Use an account with the right access, ideally one your business owns.

## Step 3: Add controls

Add a **date range control** at the top so viewers can switch between this month, last month, or a custom period. You can also add a comparison period to show change automatically.

## Step 4: Build the layout

A clear small business dashboard often looks like this:

1. **Top row: scorecards** for leads, cost per lead, sessions, and website conversion rate, each showing change from the previous period.
2. **Middle: leads by channel**, as a bar chart or table, so you can compare sources at a glance.
3. **Trend chart:** leads and sessions over time, to spot seasonality and sudden changes.
4. **Bottom: top landing pages**, showing sessions and leads for each.

Keep it to one or two pages. A dashboard nobody can read in two minutes won't get used. If different people need different details, create a summary page for owners and a second page with channel breakdowns for whoever manages campaigns.

## Step 5: Use clear labels

Rename metrics into plain language, like "Leads" instead of the technical event name. Add short text notes explaining what each section shows. Use consistent colors for each channel across charts.

## Step 6: Check the numbers

Compare a few dashboard numbers with the original platforms to make sure filters and date ranges match. Small setup mistakes, like including test traffic or the wrong conversion event, can mislead you for months.

## Step 7: Share it

Share view-only access with your team or business partners. You can also schedule the report to be emailed automatically each month, which builds a review habit.

## Keep it current

Update the dashboard when your goals change, you add a new channel, or your website changes in ways that affect tracking. A quick check every quarter keeps it trustworthy.
