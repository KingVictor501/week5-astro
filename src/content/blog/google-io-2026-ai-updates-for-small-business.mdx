Google held its annual developer conference, Google I/O, on May 20, 2026, and announced a long list of AI updates across Search, Gmail, the Gemini app, and Workspace. Not every announcement matters to a small business owner, but several change how customers find you and how you can run daily operations. Here are the Google AI tools for small business worth paying attention to.

<Callout title="TL;DR">
  Google's I/O 2026 announcements include a redesigned AI-powered Search box, background "information agents," AI draft replies in Gmail, a Daily Brief in the Gemini app, a Google Pics tool for marketing images, and a shopping cart with checkout on Google. Many features roll out gradually and require paid Google AI plans, so check availability before planning around them.
</Callout>

## What Google announced

All details below come from Google's official recap, [100 things we announced at Google I/O 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/ai/google-io-2026-all-our-announcements/). Availability varies by feature, plan, and country.

### A redesigned Search box

Google calls it "the biggest upgrade to our Search box in over 25 years." People can now search with text, images, files, videos, and Chrome tabs, and Google says the new AI Search experience is live on desktop and mobile.

**Why it matters:** as searches become more conversational and multimodal, clear, helpful content that answers specific questions becomes even more important for being found.

### Information agents in Search

Google described information agents that run "in the background 24/7 so you can stay updated on any topic, task or project that matters to you." They're rolling out this summer, starting with Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers.

**Why it matters:** owners could use them to monitor industry news or topics relevant to their business without manual searching.

### AI Inbox draft replies in Gmail

Gmail's AI Inbox can now write "personalized draft replies based on contextual information," rolling out to Google AI Plus and Pro subscribers in the U.S.

**Why it matters:** for owners buried in email, AI drafts can speed up routine replies. Review every draft before sending, especially to customers.

### Daily Brief in the Gemini app

Daily Brief "organizes and prioritizes your day ahead with a personalized digest based on your goals," reviewing your inbox, calendar, and tasks. It's rolling out to Google AI subscribers, starting in the U.S.

### Google Pics in Workspace

Google Pics is an image creation and editing tool that can make marketing assets like flyers and infographics. It's in limited testing, with a broader rollout to AI Pro and Ultra subscribers planned for this summer.

**Why it matters:** quick, on-brand graphics for social posts and promotions without a design tool.

### Universal Cart and checkout

Google introduced Universal Cart, "a truly intelligent shopping cart," with price-drop and stock alerts and checkout on Google, supported by the Universal Commerce Protocol.

**Why it matters:** retailers selling online should watch how product listings and checkout options appear in these new experiences.

### For builders: AI Studio

Google also said AI Studio now lets builders deploy their first two apps to Google Cloud at no cost, lowering the barrier for entrepreneurs experimenting with AI apps.

## What small businesses should do now

1. **Strengthen your content.** Answer real customer questions clearly on your website, which supports visibility as Search evolves.
2. **Check your Google plan.** Many features depend on paid Google AI subscriptions. Decide which, if any, are worth it for your team.
3. **Pilot one workflow.** Try AI draft replies or Daily Brief for a few weeks and measure the time saved.
4. **Keep humans in the loop** for anything customer-facing.
