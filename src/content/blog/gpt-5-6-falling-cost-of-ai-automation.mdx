On July 9, 2026, OpenAI launched **GPT-5.6**, a new family of three models called Sol, Terra, and Luna, each with its own price point. For entrepreneurs, the headline isn't just another model release. It's that capable AI is arriving in tiers, which makes it easier to match the cost of AI automation to the job at hand.

<Callout title="TL;DR">
  GPT-5.6 comes in three variants: Sol (the workhorse), Terra (intermediate), and Luna (budget). Per million tokens, OpenAI priced them at $5/$30, $2.50/$15, and $1/$6 for input/output. The practical lesson for small businesses: use cheaper tiers for simple, high-volume tasks and save premium models for complex work.
</Callout>

## What OpenAI launched

According to [TechCrunch's report on the launch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/09/openai-launches-its-new-family-of-models-with-gpt-5-6/):

- **GPT-5.6 Sol** is described as the workhorse model.
- **GPT-5.6 Terra** is an intermediate option.
- **GPT-5.6 Luna** is the budget-friendly option.
- The models are available across **ChatGPT, Codex, and the OpenAI API**.
- OpenAI also introduced **ChatGPT Work**, a workplace companion for tasks like drafting documents, spreadsheets, and presentations.

### The pricing

TechCrunch reported these API prices per million tokens:

| Model | Input | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Sol | $5 | $30 |
| Terra | $2.50 | $15 |
| Luna | $1 | $6 |

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman also told CNBC, as TechCrunch reported, that Sol is 54% more token-efficient on AI coding tasks. That's a company claim, not an independent measurement.

## Why tiered pricing matters for small businesses

Most automation tasks in a small business aren't difficult. Sorting inquiries, summarizing emails, tagging leads, drafting routine replies, and extracting details from forms are repetitive and predictable. They don't need the most powerful model available.

Tiered models let you:

- **Run high-volume, simple tasks cheaply** on a budget tier like Luna
- **Reserve premium tiers** like Sol for complex reasoning, coding, or important customer-facing writing
- **Test before scaling**, since lower costs make experiments less risky

## Matching the model to the task

A simple way to think about it:

- **Budget tier:** classifying inquiries, summarizing documents, extracting fields, short routine messages
- **Middle tier:** drafting longer content, handling more nuanced questions, multi-step workflows
- **Top tier:** complex analysis, coding, and anything where errors are costly

Start with the cheapest tier that does the job reliably, test it with real examples, and only move up when quality falls short.

## Estimating your costs

API pricing is per token, roughly a fraction of a word. To estimate:

1. Pick one workflow, such as summarizing incoming inquiries.
2. Estimate how many times it runs per month and how long each input and output is.
3. Multiply by the per-token price for the tier you plan to use.
4. Compare with the time it saves your team.

For many simple workflows, the cost is small compared with the hours saved. The bigger investment is usually setting up the workflow well.

## Questions to ask before choosing a tier

- How costly is a mistake in this task? Higher stakes justify a stronger model.
- How often does the task run? High volume favors cheaper tiers.
- Does the output reach customers directly, or does a person review it first?
- Can you measure quality with a small set of real examples before scaling?

## What doesn't change

Cheaper models don't remove the need for good process design. You still need clear instructions, human review for customer-facing output, careful handling of customer data, and monitoring to catch mistakes.
