"Is social media actually worth it?" It's a fair question for any small business owner spending hours on posts or money on ads. To measure social media ROI, you need to connect social activity to real business results like website visits, leads, and sales. Here's how to do that without expensive tools.

<Callout title="TL;DR">
  Decide which results count, tag every social link with UTM parameters, track leads and sales in Google Analytics, add up what you spend in time and ads, and compare the two. Watch engagement too, but judge success by leads and revenue.
</Callout>

## Start with the right goals

Social media ROI depends on what you want social to do. Common goals include:

- **Leads:** inquiries, bookings, and quote requests
- **Sales:** online purchases or in-store visits
- **Traffic:** visits to your website or blog
- **Awareness:** reach and follower growth among your target audience

Lead and sales goals are the easiest to tie to ROI. Awareness goals matter too, but measure them as supporting indicators rather than direct return.

## Vanity metrics vs. business metrics

Likes, followers, and impressions show reach and interest. They don't show whether social media is helping your business. Track them, but focus your decisions on:

- Website visits from social platforms
- Leads and sales from those visits
- Messages and calls that came through social
- Cost per lead from paid social campaigns

## Step 1: Tag your links with UTM parameters

UTM parameters are short tags added to the end of a link that tell Google Analytics where a visitor came from. Without them, much social traffic gets lumped together or misattributed. Google's guide to [collecting campaign data with custom URLs](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10917952) explains how they work.

A tagged link might use:

- `utm_source=instagram`
- `utm_medium=social`
- `utm_campaign=spring-offer`

Use the same naming conventions every time so reports stay clean.

## Step 2: Track conversions in Google Analytics

Set up key events in Google Analytics 4 for the actions that matter, such as form submissions, bookings, and calls. Google's guide to [setting up Analytics for a website](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9304153) covers the basics. Then use the traffic acquisition reports to see how many visits and conversions came from each social source and campaign.

## Step 3: Count leads that don't come through the website

Not every social lead clicks a link. Some message you directly or call after seeing a post. Simple habits help:

- Ask new customers how they heard about you.
- Log social media messages that become inquiries.
- Use a dedicated booking link or offer code for social followers.

## Step 4: Add up your investment

ROI compares return with cost. Include:

- **Ad spend** on social platforms
- **Time** spent planning, creating, posting, and engaging, valued at a reasonable hourly rate
- **Tools and services**, such as scheduling software or freelance help

## Step 5: Calculate ROI

The basic formula:

**ROI = (revenue from social − cost of social) ÷ cost of social**

If you don't track revenue directly, compare **cost per lead** from social with other channels, and estimate revenue using your average customer value and close rate.

## Step 6: Review monthly and adjust

Each month, look at which platforms, post types, and campaigns drove the most leads at the lowest cost. Do more of what works, cut what doesn't, and test something new each month.

## Keep perspective

Social media often plays a supporting role. Someone might discover you on Instagram, search your name later, and book through your website. That journey may not show up neatly in reports, so combine data with customer feedback.
