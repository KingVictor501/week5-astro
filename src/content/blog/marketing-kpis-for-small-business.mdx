Marketing platforms report dozens of numbers. Most of them won't change what you do next. The right marketing KPIs for small business are the handful that connect your spending to customers and revenue, and that you'll actually review every month.

<Callout title="TL;DR">
  Track eight numbers: leads by channel, cost per lead, lead-to-customer rate, customer acquisition cost, average customer value, website conversion rate, return on ad spend, and revenue by channel. Review them monthly and act on the biggest gaps.
</Callout>

## What makes a good KPI

A useful KPI is:

- **Tied to a business outcome**, like leads, customers, or revenue
- **Measurable reliably** with the tools you have
- **Actionable**, meaning a change in the number tells you what to do

Before choosing KPIs, define your goals. The U.S. Small Business Administration's guidance on [market research and competitive analysis](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/plan-your-business/market-research-competitive-analysis) is a helpful starting point for understanding your market and setting realistic targets.

## 1. Leads by channel

**What it is:** the number of inquiries, bookings, or quote requests from each marketing channel, such as organic search, paid search, social, email, and referrals.

**Why it matters:** it shows which channels produce opportunities, not just traffic. Track it in Google Analytics 4 with key events, plus call tracking and a "how did you hear about us?" question.

## 2. Cost per lead

**Formula:** marketing spend on a channel ÷ leads from that channel

**Why it matters:** it lets you compare channels fairly. A channel with fewer leads may still be the best choice if each lead costs much less.

## 3. Lead-to-customer conversion rate

**Formula:** new customers ÷ leads × 100

**Why it matters:** not all leads are equal. If one channel's leads rarely become customers, its low cost per lead may be misleading. This KPI often requires matching leads to sales in your CRM or a simple spreadsheet.

## 4. Customer acquisition cost (CAC)

**Formula:** total marketing spend ÷ new customers

**Why it matters:** it tells you what it really costs to win a customer, combining cost per lead and conversion rate into one number.

## 5. Average customer value

**What it is:** how much revenue a typical customer brings, ideally over the whole relationship rather than one purchase.

**Why it matters:** compare it with customer acquisition cost. If a customer is worth far more than it costs to acquire them, you can afford to invest more in marketing.

## 6. Website conversion rate

**Formula:** website leads ÷ website sessions × 100

**Why it matters:** it shows how well your website turns visitors into leads. A falling rate can point to a broken form, a slow page, or traffic that doesn't match your offer.

## 7. Return on ad spend (ROAS)

**Formula:** revenue from ads ÷ ad spend

**Why it matters:** for paid campaigns, it shows whether ads generate more revenue than they cost. If you can't track revenue directly, estimate it from leads, your close rate, and average customer value.

## 8. Revenue by channel

**What it is:** how much revenue each marketing channel contributes.

**Why it matters:** it's the clearest view of where your marketing pays off. Even an approximate split, based on customer source records, is valuable.

## Setting targets

Once you have a few months of data, set targets based on your own results rather than generic benchmarks. Aim to improve the KPIs that limit growth most, such as lowering cost per lead or raising the website conversion rate.

## Reviewing KPIs monthly

1. Update the numbers for the past month.
2. Compare with the previous month and the same month last year.
3. Identify the biggest change, good or bad, and find out why.
4. Decide on one or two actions.
5. Note the actions so you can measure their effect.
