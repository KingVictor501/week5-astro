"How much should I spend on Google Ads?" is one of the first questions small business owners ask, and the honest answer is: it depends on your numbers. The good news is that you can work out a sensible Google Ads budget for your small business with a little simple math and a few weeks of data.

<Callout title="TL;DR">
  Start from your goal: how many leads you need per month. Multiply by your expected cost per lead (cost per click divided by conversion rate) to get a monthly budget. Commit to it for at least two to three months, track conversions, then adjust based on real cost per lead and lead value.
</Callout>

## Start with what a customer is worth

Before choosing a number, know what a new customer is worth to you. Consider the average value of a first job or sale, how many customers come back, and your profit margin. If a new customer brings in significant profit over time, you can afford to pay more to win one than a business with one-time, low-margin sales.

## The basic budget formula

You only need three numbers:

1. **Cost per click (CPC):** what you pay, on average, for each click.
2. **Conversion rate:** the percentage of clicks that become leads.
3. **Leads needed:** how many leads you want each month.

From those:

- **Cost per lead** = CPC ÷ conversion rate
- **Monthly budget** = cost per lead × leads needed

For example, using made-up round numbers to show the math: if clicks cost $4 and 5% of visitors become leads, each lead costs $80. If you want 20 leads a month, you'd plan for about $1,600. Your real numbers will be different, so treat this as a method, not a benchmark.

## Where to find your numbers

- **Cost per click:** Google's Keyword Planner shows estimated bid ranges for your keywords and location. If you've run ads before, use your actual average CPC.
- **Conversion rate:** if you have no history, start with a conservative assumption and replace it with real data after a few weeks.
- **Leads needed:** work backward from sales goals and how many leads you typically close.

## How daily budgets work

Google Ads budgets are set per campaign as an average daily amount. On some days Google may spend more than your daily budget when there's more opportunity, but over a month it stays within your limits. Google explains this in its guide to [setting an average daily budget](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2375420). To set one, divide your monthly budget by about 30.4.

## Budget too small? Narrow your focus

If the math produces a number you can't afford, don't spread a small budget thinly. Instead:

- Focus on your most profitable service
- Target a smaller geographic area
- Run ads during your business hours, when you can answer calls
- Use tighter match types and a solid negative keyword list

A small, focused campaign usually performs better than a small budget stretched across everything.

## Give it time

The first weeks of a campaign are a learning period. Automated bidding needs conversion data, and you need enough clicks to judge keywords fairly. Plan to keep your budget steady for at least two to three months before making big decisions. Changing it every few days makes results harder to read.

## When to increase your budget

Increase spending when:

- Your cost per lead is comfortably below what a lead is worth
- Campaigns are limited by budget and losing impressions
- You can handle more leads without hurting service

Raise budgets gradually so you can see the effect, and keep watching cost per lead as you scale.
