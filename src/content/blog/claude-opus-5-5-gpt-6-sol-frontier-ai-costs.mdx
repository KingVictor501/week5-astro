September 22, 2026 brought two significant AI releases on the same day. Anthropic introduced **Claude Opus 5.5**, and OpenAI launched **GPT-6 Sol and Luna**. Both companies emphasized the same theme: more capable models at lower prices. For small businesses weighing AI model pricing, it's a good moment to revisit what's affordable.

<Callout title="TL;DR">
  Anthropic says Claude Opus 5.5 costs 40% less to run than Opus 5 on typical workloads, at $4/$20 per million input/output tokens. OpenAI says GPT-6 Sol and Luna cost half as much through the API as the GPT-5.6 versions. If you ruled out an AI workflow on cost earlier this year, it may be worth recalculating.
</Callout>

## What Anthropic announced

According to Anthropic's [Claude Opus 5.5 announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5):

- Claude Opus 5.5 is the **first model in the Claude 5.5 family**, with Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 expected in the following weeks.
- Anthropic says it **costs 40% less to run** than Opus 5 on typical workloads.
- Pricing is **$4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens**, 20% below Opus 5.
- Anthropic states that "it performs at the level of Claude Fable 5.1 on most work."
- It's available on Anthropic's platform and through **Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Azure**.
- **Zero data retention** is available, which matters for businesses handling sensitive information.

## What OpenAI announced

According to [TechCrunch's report on GPT-6 Sol and Luna](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/22/openai-launches-gpt-6-sol-and-luna/):

- **GPT-6 Sol** targets complex work, such as coding.
- **GPT-6 Luna** targets high-volume, clearly defined tasks, like summarizing documents or answering quick questions.
- **API pricing is half** that of the GPT-5.6 Sol and Luna models, which OpenAI attributes to improvements in caching and inference.
- OpenAI claims GPT-6 Sol makes about half as many mistakes as its predecessor.
- The models are available in ChatGPT Work, Codex, and the API, with Luna also available to Free and Go users.

Performance and cost figures are the companies' own claims.

## What this means for small businesses

### Recalculate shelved ideas

If you explored an AI workflow earlier in 2026 and decided it cost too much, run the numbers again. Lower per-token prices can turn a marginal idea into a clear win, especially for high-volume tasks.

### Match tiers to tasks

Both releases continue the trend of tiered models. Use lighter, cheaper models for routine, high-volume tasks and reserve the most capable ones for complex reasoning or important writing.

### Look beyond price

Cost is only one factor. Also consider:

- **Data handling:** options like zero data retention and where your data is processed
- **Integration:** whether a model works with the tools and cloud platforms you already use
- **Reliability:** test each model with your real tasks, since benchmark claims don't always match everyday results
- **Consistency:** pick a model you can standardize on for a workflow, rather than switching constantly

## Watch for hidden costs

Per-token prices are only part of the bill. Longer prompts, retries, and verbose outputs all add up, and the time spent setting up and reviewing a workflow often matters more than the model price itself. Track actual monthly spend for the first few weeks rather than relying on estimates alone.

## A simple way to compare

1. Choose one real workflow, such as summarizing customer inquiries.
2. Run the same set of examples through two or three models.
3. Compare quality, speed, and estimated monthly cost.
4. Pick the least expensive option that meets your quality bar.
